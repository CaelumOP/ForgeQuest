# Exercise difficulty tiers (step 1 of the muscle → exercises plan)

Rule: how hard the movement is to learn and its leverage, not the load (you choose the weight).
- **Foundation:** floor-, bench- or machine-supported, short lever, easy to learn.
- **Intermediate:** full bodyweight moves and free-weight compounds that need bracing or balance.
- **Advanced:** skills and high-leverage moves; weighted pull-ups and dips (after bodyweight ones are easy).

## Ladder check for step E ("ready for a harder version")
Step E only applies to bodyweight rep moves; weighted moves already progress by adding load. These bodyweight moves
have no harder version in the same movement with the same equipment yet:
- **Squat (bodyweight, half-rep squats):** no Intermediate two-leg squat. Next step crosses to one-leg work: lunge → Bulgarian split squat (a chair works).
- **Australian rows / chin-ups (Foundation):** no Intermediate horizontal pull. Next step crosses to vertical pulling: negative pull-up → pull-up.
- **Hip bridge, calf raise, crunch, Russian twist, superman hold, tibialis raise:** no harder bodyweight version yet (e.g. single-leg bridge, single-leg calf raise). These are part of the later new-exercises update (D).
- **Diamond push-up, hollow body hold, burpee (Intermediate):** no Advanced step.

Proposed handling: allow one "related movement" hop (two-leg squat → one-leg squat, horizontal pull → vertical pull),
always within the user's equipment. Where there's still no harder move, the note suggests a slower tempo instead
("3 seconds down, 1 second pause") rather than a swap.
